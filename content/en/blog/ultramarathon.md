Tomorrow will be my first attempt to do a 50km run, I am not running alone, I invited people and I am grateful that some of them is
joining even for a short part.
Shared pain makes the burden less heavy, that's what I learnt during my dark days.

## The People Who Ask You to Stay

There is a scene in *One Piece* that has stayed with me for years.

Robin had spent most of her life running. After Ohara, she became someone who expected to be abandoned. She learned to survive alone, never allowing herself to believe that she could truly belong somewhere.

Then, at Enies Lobby, the Straw Hats gave her something she had never allowed herself to ask for.

A reason to live.

When Robin finally screams, **“I want to live!”**, it isn't just a declaration that she wants to survive. It's the moment she accepts that she is allowed to want a future.

Sometimes, when we're at our lowest, we convince ourselves that we are completely alone. That nobody would notice if we disappeared. That there is nothing waiting for us on the other side of the pain.

I know that feeling.

But sometimes the people who keep us here aren't the people we expect. Sometimes it's a friend who answers the phone. A family member who shows up. Someone who runs a few kilometres beside you.

And sometimes, we simply need someone to tell us:
**You are not a burden. You are allowed to stay.**
That's what the Straw Hats did for Robin.
And, in my own way, that's what those people did for me.

## The Last Call

One thing we have to accept is that life and the world itself isn't fair. Sometimes, the pain becomes so unbearable that the only thing you can think about is ending it all.
I was there too.
When you lose everything you have and feel the weight of loneliness, you can lose faith in tomorrow and, more dangerously,the respect you have for yourself.

Back then, my hope was like the last ember of a dying fire, slowly losing its light.
That day was supposed to be my last call—to my friends, my aunt, and the people I loved. I was crying, with nowhere to go. I was broke as hell, with no home to return to. I was heartbroken. My career had become a series of minimum-wage jobs with no clear prospect of a better future. And, perhaps hardest of all, I felt completely unsupported by my own family.

I thought that call would be my last.
Maybe, in a way, it was.

It was the last call for the version of me who existed in that moment.
Something inside me died in the misery I wanted to leave behind. Something I wanted to bury once and for all.
But another version of me was waiting to emerge.
One who decided to treat life—and every single day—as if it were his last.


> I was lucky, but not all people have this kind of support

## Dying for love

One of my cousins died by suicide after a breakup.
She was young, with so much love left to give and so much life still ahead of her. But in her darkest moment, the pain became greater than what she believed she could carry.

The love that once made her feel alive became intertwined with a loss she couldn't see beyond.
Her story is one of the reasons I run.

Not because heartbreak is something we can simply outrun, or because running can heal every wound. But because I want to believe that no one should have to face their darkest moment believing it is the end of their story.


Tomorrow, I'll run 50 kilometres carrying these stories with me.
Not to outrun them. Not to pretend that running can fix them.
But to remind myself that there is still another kilometre ahead.
